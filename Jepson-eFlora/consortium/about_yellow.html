Yellow Flags

In recent years, many California herbaria have digitized over 2 million specimen records in the Consortium of California Herbaria (CCH). Sixty-five percent are georeferenced, making data available for researchers studying climate change and conservation.

When seeking to understand a taxon's range, we computationally combine the Jepson eFlora range descriptions with georeferenced specimen distribution. When a specimen's location falls outside the range described in the eFlora, it is tagged with a "yellow flag."

Case Study: Xylococcus bicolor

Analysis revealed many specimens in the Peninsular Ranges (PR) that weren't originally in the eFlora range statement. Author confirmation resulted in adding PR to the distribution, resolving the flags.

Before: Yellow flags outside original range.

After: Range updated to include PR.

Why are flags generated?

  • Incorrect Coordinates: Transcription or transcription errors in latitude/longitude.
  • Mapping Errors: False positives near boundaries or very small region units (e.g., offshore islets).
  • Misidentification: Specimens incorrectly keyed or taxonomic revisions not yet applied.

Get Involved: We welcome input from field botanists to help resolve these flags. Please contact Staci Markos (smarkos@berkeley.edu) or submit comments directly through the CCH portal.
